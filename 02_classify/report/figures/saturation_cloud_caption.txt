FULL CAPTION

Every species-rank taxon Kraken2 assigned a read to in each sample, placed by how much
of that taxon's k-mer space the reads could have covered against how much they did.
Both axes are normalised by the taxon's own total in db_v4, so taxa with genomes
differing by four orders of magnitude are directly comparable on one panel:

  x  redundancy = reads x k-mers per spot / the taxon's db_v4 minimizers
  y  coverage   = distinct minimizers observed / the same total

THE CURVE IS THE NULL, y = 1 - exp(-x): where a taxon would sit if the reads assigned
to it were spread uniformly across its k-mer space. It is the same curve for every
taxon in every sample, which is what normalising buys. Saturation, the statistic in
_reports.py, is the vertical distance from it.

REAL CALLS SIT BELOW THE CURVE, typically at 0.1-0.6 of it, and that is expected rather
than a failure: the null assumes uniform sampling and RNA-seq expression is not uniform,
so reads concentrate on highly expressed genes. The curve is a CEILING on what uniform
sampling could reach. What matters is that leakage sits two further orders of magnitude
beneath it.

Measured on PRJEB33109, 4 runs, 63 taxa:
  clears 1% coverage         n= 9   median saturation 0.2795   range 0.0700 - 0.5595
  leakage (>=1k reads, <1%)  n=54   median saturation 0.0017   range 0.0001 - 0.0457
No overlap, and the separation holds per run (241x, 359x, 230x, 162x) BECAUSE the null
scales with read count. A raw reads-per-distinct ratio does NOT work and was tried: its
separation decayed 20.8x to 1.0x across those same four runs as depth rose, since a
deeply sequenced real organism accumulates redundant reads too.

WHAT THE FALLEN POINTS ARE. Not contamination and not a classifier error, but REFERENCE
INCOMPLETENESS. A read from a host transcript absent from db_v4's CDS for that host,
whose ortholog in a related species IS present, has only the relative's k-mers to match
and takes the best home available; were both present the LCA would resolve it to the
shared ancestor instead. The clearest case: 99,509 reads assigned to Oryza sativa in a
wheat sample, saturation 0.0019 -- real rice would have produced ~6.6 M distinct
minimizers, not 24,388.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY. Saturation is measured, not yet calibrated: the separation above
comes from four wheat runs of a single Puccinia-dominated project, and no threshold is
set on it here. The floors drawn are the published operating point (1% coverage, and an
absolute 10,000 distinct minimizers alongside it, since 1% of db_v4's smallest
species is 29 k-mers and reachable by cross-mapping alone).

SHORT CAPTION

Species-rank taxa per sample, positioned by k-mer redundancy against observed coverage,
both normalised by the taxon's db_v4 total so all taxa share one scale. The curve is the
null for reads spread uniformly over a taxon's k-mer space. Points near it behave like a
real organism; points far below it are reads piled onto a few loci, the signature of a
gap in the reference for the true source taxon.
