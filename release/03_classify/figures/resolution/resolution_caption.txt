FULL CAPTION

db2 is db1p plus 135 gene-predicted genomes and nothing else, so every
difference between the arms is attributable to those references. (a) is the
result in three numbers: the net FUNGAL READS that come to rest at each rank
in db2 against db1p, summed over 1,560 runs, with the number of runs moving
that way under each bar. (b) is the evidence that those totals are not a
handful of deep libraries: one curve per rank, the cumulative distribution
over the same runs of each run's change in the SHARE of its fungal reads
resting there. A curve lying wholly to one side of the dotted zero line means
the direction held in every run; where a curve crosses 0.5 is the median run.

THE READS MOVE UP, not down, and that is the predictions working rather than
failing. species: median 71.05% to 70.85%, a change of -0.188 points in the
median run, share up in 173 runs and down in 1,387, -43,734,229 reads across
the cohort. genus: median 1.44% to 1.68%, a change of +0.186 points in the
median run, share up in 1,490 runs and down in 69, +68,592,385 reads across
the cohort. above genus: median 23.96% to 23.95%, a change of +0.001 points in
the median run, share up in 881 runs and down in 679, +2,275,034 reads across
the cohort.

THE TWO PANELS COUNT DIFFERENT THINGS and their run counts therefore differ:
(a) counts reads, (b) counts each rank's share of a run's fungal reads, and a
run can gain reads at a rank while that rank's share of its reads falls. Genus
gains reads in 1,531 of 1,560 runs and gains share in 1,490.

The worklist is three tiers and they predict different directions, which is
why the pooled movement has to be read against what was built: tier A is 118
genomes already in the reference scope with no gene model, present in the
database and uncallable, so giving them CDS puts a COMPETITOR in a genus the
database already holds; tier C2 is 14 deliberate competitors for a callable
taxon that had none, whose intended effect is reads moving UP to the genus
node so that Kraken2's LCA can decline to name a species; and tier B is 3
genomes in a genus the database did not hold at all, which is the only tier
the question 'do reads move DOWN to species' is about. 132 of the 135
therefore predict exactly the movement the figure shows.

So the result is ARTEFACT REMOVAL rather than resolution gain. Calls fall from
6,629 to 6,380 across the cohort, the host aside: residents 4,402 to 4,209,
wheat pathogens 2,185 to 2,159, plant pathogens 42 to 12. The clearest single
case is Stemphylium lycopersici, a pathogen of another plant, called in 21
runs under db1p and in 1 under db2: its reads were Pleosporaceae reads with
nowhere better to go, and with Alternaria triticina in the database they go
there instead. The predicted taxa are competitors only and can never
themselves be a call, in either arm, so a detection can never rest on our own
gene prediction.

THE X AXIS IS SYMMETRIC-LOG. Changes span three orders of magnitude in both
directions and include exact zero, so the axis is linear within 0.05 of a
percentage point of zero and logarithmic outside it. That is why the curves
stand up steeply near the middle: most runs change by a few hundredths of a
point, and the handful that change by tens of points are the tails.

Fungal reads only. The host is most of a plant RNA-seq library and resolves at
species, so it sets the all-reads denominator and buries any fungal movement
below the second decimal place; the restriction is a lineage test on the Fungi
node, not a name match. All 1,560 runs classified in both arms are drawn,
rather than the field-wheat set the other panels narrow to, because the
question here is about the database and every run is evidence about it.
