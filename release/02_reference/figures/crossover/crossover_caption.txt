FULL CAPTION

Pairwise minimizer sharing among the 254 fungal taxa the USDA Plant-Fungal
Database records on wheat (the wheat row of the reference), spanning 90 genera
and built from 1,289 genome assemblies. For each taxon the set of canonical
minimizers of its coding sequence was computed (length 31 over 35-base
windows, the db1p scheme), unioned across its assemblies; a cell is the exact
intersection of two such sets. Colour is crossover, the shared count divided
by the smaller taxon's total, so a cell reads as the fraction of the smaller
taxon's minimizers the two share: 0 (white) means the classifier can tell them
apart, 1 means it cannot. The diagonal is masked.

Rows and columns are ordered and bracketed by the NCBI taxonomy (phylum to
species) drawn as a cladogram on the top and left; radius encodes rank, not
divergence. The strip on each axis colours every taxon by its panel-A type:
204 recorded on wheat with no pathogenicity record (green), 25 pathogens of
other plants (amber), and 25 wheat pathogens (orange-red), using the
matrix-figure palette.

Crossover is confined to the diagonal blocks. Mean crossover between congeners
(same genus) is 0.015; between taxa in different genera it is 0.0001,
effectively zero at this minimizer length. Sharing is therefore a within-genus
phenomenon: a read that cannot be resolved to species is nonetheless resolved
to genus, and no cross-genus taxon absorbs it.

The confusions are species complexes, not database errors: the bunt pair
(Tilletia), the Fusarium head-blight group, and the Alternaria, Pyricularia
and Ustilago complexes. The three wheat rusts resolve cleanly and are NOT
highlighted (Puccinia graminis, Puccinia striiformis, Puccinia triticina), the
separability that lets the classifier distinguish stripe, leaf and stem rust
despite their shared genus.

The largest single off-diagonal count is 8,825,770 minimizers, between
Alternaria alternata and Alternaria tenuissima.
