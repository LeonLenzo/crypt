FULL CAPTION

Geographic distribution of fungal pathogens detected in 928 classified wheat libraries
that carry field coordinates, across 384 localities in 35 countries. Each panel is one
species, and a locality is marked only where that species was detected in a library whose
study DID NOT declare it, so every coloured point is an unreported detection.

That restriction matters rather than being a formality. Puccinia triticina is the declared
target of one project and an undeclared bystander in eight others; mapping both together
would draw the sampling design instead of the biology.

OPEN GREY POINTS ARE EVERY SAMPLED LOCALITY and are repeated in all panels. A detection
map without its denominator shows where people sampled, which for wheat is overwhelmingly
northwest Europe, and an empty region could mean nobody looked. The grey layer is what
makes an absence readable.

THE MAP LOCATES, IT DOES NOT MEASURE. Point density follows sampling effort: a point is a
locality where a species was found, and more libraries make a find more likely. For how
OFTEN a species occurs, which has a denominator, see the per-country rates in
geography.tsv. The latitude of detections is the clearest illustration, since the median
absolute latitude of every sampled locality is 49.6 degrees:

Microdochium nivale looks purely northern on its panel, which invites reading snow mould
as a cold-climate signal. It is not distinguishable from the sampling baseline. The two
species that DO sit away from it are Cladosporium cladosporioides and Epicoccum nigrum,
both lower-latitude and both phyllosphere saprotrophs that FUNGuild also labels plant
pathogens; neither is drawn.

POINT SIZE is the number of libraries at that locality in which the species was detected,
not a rate. Most localities contributed a single library, so a rate there would be 0 or
100% and would overstate what one sample can say.

COORDINATE HANDLING. 140 of 1,068 classified wheat libraries are not drawn:
   129  no coordinates
    10  precision 'country'
     1  precision 'region'
Libraries whose location resolves only to a country or region are excluded rather than
placed at a centroid. On a map about where disease was found, a centroid invents a field
site at a place nobody sampled.

WHAT THIS IS NOT. Every library was collected because the plant was diseased, so these
are not prevalence surfaces. A locality with no coloured point was sampled and the species
was not detected there, which under a disease-conditioned design is weaker than absence.

SHORT CAPTION

Undeclared fungal pathogens across 384 wheat sampling localities in 35 countries.
Each panel is one species; coloured points mark localities where it was detected in a
library whose study did not report it, sized by the number of such libraries. Open grey
points are every sampled locality and are repeated in each panel, so an empty region can
be read as sampled-and-not-found rather than unsampled. Detections are conditioned on
visible disease and are not prevalence estimates.
