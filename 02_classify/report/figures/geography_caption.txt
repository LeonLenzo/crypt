FULL CAPTION

Fungal pathogens detected in 527 wheat leaf libraries from PRJEB39201, a single
field surveillance programme that collected yellow-rust-infected leaves across
29 countries between 2014 and 2018 under one protocol. The study reported
Puccinia striiformis and no other pathogen, so every further species here is unreported by
construction, and because the protocol is constant a difference between countries is not a
difference between studies.

PANEL a. Detection rate across all libraries. P. striiformis, the declared pathogen, is
recovered in 527 of 527 libraries (100.0%), which is the positive control for
the reference database and the detection floors. The species below it were not reported by
the original study.

PANEL b. Per-country detection rate for the two most frequent unreported pathogens, in
countries with at least 8 libraries. The two gradients run opposite ways and both
reproduce independently known disease geography: Zymoseptoria tritici, the dominant wheat
pathogen of maritime northwest Europe, is highest in the Netherlands, France, the UK and
Poland and falls away in Mediterranean, continental and African sites, while Puccinia
triticina peaks in Argentina and is absent from the Ethiopian highlands, where yellow and
stem rust dominate. Neither pattern was supplied to the pipeline, which was given no
geographic information of any kind.

DEPTH, which had to be addressed before any of this could be read as spatial. Sequencing
depth expressed as G_kmer spans a factor of 300 across the cohort and covaries with crop,
which is why this work makes no comparison between crops. Within wheat the correlation
between a country's median G_kmer and its co-infection rate is
r = -0.053 over 18 countries across all projects, and the deepest libraries are among the
lowest-rate ones. Within THIS project alone it is r = +0.271 over 9 countries, which is weak,
of the opposite sign, and underpowered: nine points would need |r| near 0.67 to reach
significance. That neither establishes a depth effect nor excludes one. What supports the
spatial reading is therefore the agreement with independently known disease geography and
the opposite direction of the two gradients, not the depth correlation. More countries with
enough libraries is the fix, and the remaining cohort supplies them.

TIME IS NOT SHOWN, and the reason is a composition artefact worth stating. Pooled by
collection year the co-infection rate rises monotonically, but the countries sampled change
with the year, and 14 of the 17 libraries collected in 2018 come from two Argentinian
sites. Holding country constant the trend largely dissolves. The year table is written to
geography.tsv for inspection and is not plotted.

WHAT THIS IS NOT. A rate from this compendium is NOT a prevalence estimate: every library
was collected because the plant was visibly rusted, so the denominator is conditioned on
disease. The quantity measured here is the rate at which a SECOND pathogen accompanies a
known one, which is a different and narrower claim.

CONGENER INFLATION IS PRESENT AND IS NOT IN THESE COUNTS. Around 120 libraries call ten or
more Alternaria species simultaneously, every one at 4.9 to 5.9% coverage and 0.10 to 0.11
saturation. Genuine co-infection by ten congeners would not produce near-identical values;
one organism's reads distributed across near-identical references does. The uniformity is
the diagnostic. Those species carry no pathogen guild assignment and so do not enter the
counts above, but the pattern is why congeneric clusters are reported as a flag throughout
this work and why competitive read assignment is the next step.

THE PHYLLOSPHERE RESIDENTS ARE DETECTED AS RESIDENTS, which is the reference database
working as designed. Cladosporium, Dioszegia and Sporobolomyces appear in 225 to 284
libraries at 4.8 to 7.4% coverage. These are the wheat leaf core community, and they were
added to the database specifically so that their reads would have somewhere correct to go
rather than being assigned to the nearest pathogen present.

Unreported fungal pathogens in 527 yellow-rust-infected wheat leaf libraries from a
single surveillance programme spanning 29 countries. (a) Detection rate across all
libraries; the declared pathogen P. striiformis is recovered in 100% as a positive control
