FULL CAPTION

Composition of the reference database: 5,843 fungal taxa carrying 21,413
genome assemblies, of which 5,302 have a gene model and can contribute CDS.
Each taxon is placed by two independent facts and the matrix crosses them.
Rows are what the USDA Plant-Fungal Database records: a host on wheat, a host
on some other plant only, or no record of the taxon itself, in which case it
entered because its genus qualified (Congener). Columns are what PHI-base
scores: no pathogenicity record, a named disease on some plant, or a named
disease on wheat. Cells are disjoint and sum to the cohort.

Each cell reports three counts. Taxa is distinct NCBI taxa; Asm. is the genome
assemblies available for them; CDS is the subset of those assemblies carrying
a gene model, which is what the database can actually be built from. The gap
between Asm. and CDS is the annotation bottleneck that motivates gene
prediction.

Colour carries both axes, so the panel needs no key. Hue is the column: green
for no pathogenicity record, amber for a pathogen of other plants, orange-red
for a wheat pathogen. Saturation is the row: full strength where USDA records
the taxon on wheat, pale where it records it only on other plants, grey for a
congener. Panel B uses the same five colours for the same five cells.


WHAT THE EMPTY CELLS MEAN

Three of the nine combinations do not occur and are NOT DRAWN. The staircase
is a result, not a layout choice, and the two gaps say different things.

No wheat pathogen in scope is off-wheat in USDA: all 25 sit in the top row.
USDA host records and PHI-base pathogenicity scores are independent
compilations made for different purposes, so their agreeing here is an
empirical check on the scope rule rather than a definition, and it is the test
the design has to pass.

No congener is a pathogen of anything, so the whole congener row is one cell
of 3,465 taxa. This is structural. A fungus PHI-base scores with a named
disease is a studied fungus, and USDA records studied fungi, so the pathogen
annotation can never reach a taxon admitted only because its genus qualified.
No detection can be labelled a pathogen on genus membership alone.


HOW THE TWO AXES ARE DEFINED

The unit is a TAXON, not a described species, and the panel says so. 1,373 of
the 3,465 congeners are NCBI `sp.` nodes: unidentified isolates carrying a
strain code and no binomial. They are legitimate reference entries, because an
unidentified Alternaria gives the classifier as real a competitor as a named
one, but they are not species in any taxonomic sense. The observation rows do
not have the problem (14 of 2,378), because USDA indexes only formal names.

A PHI-base row counts only where it NAMES A DISEASE. PHI-base scores an
interaction against whatever host the experiment used, and the plant is not
always the host being harmed: in a tripartite insect-fungus-plant assay, or an
endophyte colonisation study, the plant is present but is not the victim, and
those rows carry Disease_name = "no data found". 5 fungi in this reference
were labelled plant pathogens on such rows alone and are not labelled here
(Aspergillus ochraceus, Epichloe festucae, Leptographium clavigerum,
Metarhizium acridum, Metarhizium robertsii). Each is a plant associate rather
than a pathogen. The condition applies to the wheat column too, where it
changes nothing.

The wheat pathogen label covers 26 taxa: 18 from PHI-base and 8 from a curated
supplement (data/inputs/wheat_pathogens_curated.tsv) carrying the canonical
wheat diseases PHI-base does not curate. 25 of them are in the reference. The
remaining 1 (Gaeumannomyces graminis) has no in-scope assembly, so it carries
the label but is not in the reference and does not appear in the panel.
Provenance is recorded per taxon in wheat_pathogen_source; no call is
anonymous.

Before counting, 16 provisional NCBI names were folded onto the USDA binomial
they belong to and 1 was promoted in place, moving 63 assemblies. USDA and
NCBI can hold one fungus under two names, which otherwise splits it into an
observation with no genome and a congener with no host record. 5 `aff.` nodes
were deliberately NOT merged, because `aff.` denotes a related undescribed
species and those belong in the congener row. Every decision is a row in
data/curation/screen_merges.tsv.


WHAT IS NOT SHOWN

Scope is ANY plant, never wheat. The database deliberately holds fungi with no
wheat record and no pathogenicity record, because a reference absent from the
database is invisible: its reads go silently to the nearest taxon present. The
columns are annotations applied to a detection, never filters on the database.

The panel counts what the reference CONTAINS, not what was detected. No cell
is a claim about abundance, prevalence or co-infection.
