FULL CAPTION

Every one of the 1,379 RNA-seq runs in the cohort is one column, grouped by
country and ordered west to east; within a country, columns run by longitude.
40 countries are represented across 24 BioProjects, 1,062 runs carry
coordinates and 32 carry no location and sit in the block at the right. Rows
are every fungus called in at least 10 runs, banded by two different rules,
because the two halves of the panel answer to different things. A wheat
pathogen is banded by the DISEASE it causes, which is the distinction a plant
pathologist makes and which no column of the reference table carries.
Everything else is banded by its SLOT IN THE REFERENCE MATRIX, read straight
off that table, so the band says exactly what the colour says and no taxon is
placed by opinion. The plant host is not drawn, because every library has one
and no study declares it as a pathogen.

The slot rule puts Microdochium majus with the taxa recorded on wheat rather
than with the stem-base diseases, and that disagreement is worth reading
rather than smoothing. It is a genuine agent of Microdochium patch, but
PHI-base does not score it, so the label says resident. The reads do not: it
is called without M. nivale in 43 of its 76 runs and at 1.46 times its
coverage where both occur, which is a detection in its own right. The gap is
in the label, not the call.

The resident layer is the larger part of what is there: 67 resident taxa are
called, 28 of them often enough for a row, led by C. allicinum 556, S. roseus
444, D. hungarica 353 runs. Alternaria is drawn as ONE row rather than one per
species. Within a run its species are called at a median coverage CV of 0.25
against 0.38 to 0.92 for every other genus called as a cluster, and a run
calling it calls a median of 12 at once: that is one organism spread over
near-identical references, not 12 co-infecting congeners. The genus also
contains wheat pathogens, and the point of the collapse is that these calls
cannot be resolved to them. 04_align is what separates a cluster.

Pathogens of other plants but not of wheat are almost absent: 5 taxa and 12
calls in total, the commonest in 7 runs, so none reaches a row. That is the
db2 arm working. Under db1' these were the conspicuous artefact, reads from a
Pleosporaceae with no close reference being forced onto a tomato or soybean
pathogen; adding the predicted competitors gave them somewhere nearer to go.

Colour carries two things and neither is abundance. The HUE is the taxon's
category in the reference matrix, unchanged from that figure: orange a wheat
pathogen, green a non-pathogen USDA records on wheat, pale green one it
records on another plant, grey a congener with no record of its own. The
TREATMENT is who knew: full colour where the study that generated the run did
not declare that organism, charcoal where it did, and a washed-out tint where
nobody has yet curated what the study declared. Counting all 14 wheat
pathogens and not only the 9 given a row, there are 1,168 declared calls, 732
undeclared and 142 of unknown reporting status. 519 runs carry at least one
undeclared wheat pathogen, and in 0 of them the undeclared pathogen is the
only one present. So the undeclared organism is a second pathogen beside the
declared one rather than an alternative to it, which follows from the declared
targets being recovered in nearly every run and is stated here as a
consequence, not a separate finding.

The unit is the run and not the BioProject, deliberately. Projects in this
cohort are not geographically coherent — the largest spans twelve countries —
so grouping by project averages a worldwide survey into one value and hides
the axis along which the complex actually varies.

That axis is visible without any statistics. Z. tritici is called in Ireland
93%, Netherlands 62%, France 61%, UK 34%, Germany 27%, and in USA 5%,
Argentina 8%, South Africa 0%, Ethiopia 11%, China 0%, Australia 0%: 39% of
617 runs from maritime northwest Europe against 5% of 408 runs from the drier
and more continental sampling, which is the published distribution of Septoria
tritici blotch. The pattern is recovered from reads collected for other
reasons, and it is evidence the calls are infections rather than
contamination: neither spore drift, index hopping nor cross-mapping is
organised by continent.

One taxon is withheld by a uniform rule. Z. brevis is called in 24 runs, 24 of
them also carrying Z. tritici, at a median 4% of its coverage: never found
alone and never more than a trace beside an abundant congener, which is k-mers
bleeding off a neighbour rather than a detection. The rule is applied to every
genus and catches only this one. The Alternaria cluster does not trip it,
because its members sit at comparable coverage to each other, which is why
that one is collapsed instead; Cladosporium inversicolor does not trip it
either, because it is called with no Cladosporium congener at all in 4 runs,
and being found alone is the evidence of independence.

Detection is presence, not activity, and a species-level call inside a densely
represented genus is a call on that genus' k-mer space; both are taken up in
04_align. Column order, project, country, coordinates and call count per run
are in samples_order.tsv.
