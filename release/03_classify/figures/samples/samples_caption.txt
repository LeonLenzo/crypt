FULL CAPTION

Every one of the 1,379 RNA-seq runs in the cohort is one column, grouped into
11 regions and ordered west to east; within a region, columns run by
longitude. The regions are cut for wheat pathology rather than by continent,
because the split that matters inside Europe is maritime northwest against
continental centre-east against Mediterranean, which is the climate axis the
foliar diseases follow; the mapping is in data/inputs/country_regions.tsv and
a country missing from it stops the figure rather than falling into an Other
bucket. 40 countries are represented across 24 BioProjects, 1,062 runs carry
coordinates and 32 carry no location and sit in the block at the right. 1
region holding 7 runs is drawn but too narrow to name; samples_order.tsv gives
the country and region of every column. Rows are every fungus called in at
least 10 runs, banded by two different rules, because the two halves of the
panel answer to different things. A wheat pathogen is banded by the DISEASE it
causes, which is the distinction a plant pathologist makes and which no column
of the reference table carries. Everything else is banded by its SLOT IN THE
REFERENCE MATRIX, read straight off that table, so the band says exactly what
the colour says and no taxon is placed by opinion. The plant host is not
drawn, because every library has one and no study declares it as a pathogen.

Taxa with no USDA host record of their own are not drawn. They are in the
reference only because their genus qualified, so there is no slot for them and
no statement this panel can make about where they belong. That costs 2 rows:
D. hungarica (353), S. ruberrimus (57). The first is named in 02_reference's
own scope notes as part of the wheat resident core, so these calls are not
being judged false, only left out of a figure whose every other row carries a
host record; supp_data_s5 keeps all of them.

The slot rule puts Microdochium majus with the taxa recorded on wheat rather
than with the stem-base diseases, and that disagreement is worth reading
rather than smoothing. It is a genuine agent of Microdochium patch, but
PHI-base does not score it, so the label says resident. The reads do not: it
is called without M. nivale in 43 of its 76 runs and at 1.46 times its
coverage where both occur, which is a detection in its own right. The gap is
in the label, not the call.

The resident layer is the larger part of what is there: 67 resident taxa are
called, 28 of them often enough for a row, led by C. allicinum 556, S. roseus
444, D. hungarica 353 runs. Alternaria is drawn as ONE row rather than one per
species. Within a run its species are called at a median coverage CV of 0.25
against 0.38 to 0.92 for every other genus called as a cluster, and a run
calling it calls a median of 12 at once: that is one organism spread over
near-identical references, not 12 co-infecting congeners. The genus also
contains wheat pathogens, and the point of the collapse is that these calls
cannot be resolved to them. 04_align is what separates a cluster.

Pathogens of other plants but not of wheat are almost absent: 5 taxa and 12
calls in total, the commonest in 7 runs, so none reaches a row. That is the
db2 arm working. Under db1' these were the conspicuous artefact, reads from a
Pleosporaceae with no close reference being forced onto a tomato or soybean
pathogen; adding the predicted competitors gave them somewhere nearer to go.

Colour carries two things and neither is abundance. The HUE is the taxon's
category in the reference matrix, unchanged from that figure: orange a wheat
pathogen, green a non-pathogen USDA records on wheat, pale green one it
records on another plant. The TREATMENT is who knew: full colour where the
study that generated the run did not declare that organism, charcoal where it
did, and a washed-out tint where nobody has yet curated what the study
declared. Counting all 14 wheat pathogens and not only the 9 given a row,
there are 1,168 declared calls, 732 undeclared and 142 of unknown reporting
status. 519 runs carry at least one undeclared wheat pathogen, and in 0 of
them the undeclared pathogen is the only one present. So the undeclared
organism is a second pathogen beside the declared one rather than an
alternative to it, which follows from the declared targets being recovered in
nearly every run and is stated here as a consequence, not a separate finding.

The unit is the run and not the BioProject, deliberately. Projects in this
cohort are not geographically coherent — the largest spans twelve countries —
so grouping by project averages a worldwide survey into one value and hides
the axis along which the complex actually varies.

That axis is visible without any statistics. Z. tritici is called in Northwest
Europe 40%, and in North America 5%, South America 7%, Southern Africa 0%,
East Africa 9%, East Asia 0%, Oceania 0%: 40% of 548 runs from maritime
northwest Europe against 4% of 494 runs from the drier and more continental
regions, which is the published distribution of Septoria tritici blotch. The
pattern is recovered from reads collected for other reasons, and it is
evidence the calls are infections rather than contamination: neither spore
drift, index hopping nor cross-mapping is organised by continent.

One row carries a dagger, and it is a flag rather than a removal. Z. brevis is
called in 24 runs, 24 of them also carrying Z. tritici, at a median 4% of its
coverage and tracking it across runs: never found alone, never more than a
trace beside an abundant congener. The rule is applied to every genus and
marks only this one. The Alternaria cluster does not trip it, its members
sitting at comparable coverage to each other, which is why that one is
collapsed instead; Cladosporium inversicolor does not either, being called
with no congener at all in 4 runs, and being found alone is the evidence of
independence.

The flagged call is NOT k-mers shared between the two references, and the
distinction decides how it can be handled. Kraken2 assigns every minimizer two
taxa share to their common ancestor, so in a passing run the Zymoseptoria
genus node absorbs about 1.29 million of them while Z. brevis still holds some
100,000 exclusive to it in this database, which cannot have come from the Z.
tritici reference. The likely mechanism is divergence from the reference
genome: field Z. tritici differs from the assembly the database was built on,
and where a field variant coincides with the brevis reference the k-mer is
counted for brevis. No coverage floor separates that, the reads being real and
the k-mers genuinely exclusive, so it is disclosed rather than thresholded
away. It is the close-relative false positive that TIPP-SD (Shen et al. 2026)
measures and leaves uncorrected, and release/04_align is what resolves it,
from species-unique reads and positional evenness.

Detection is presence, not activity, and a species-level call inside a densely
represented genus is a call on that genus' k-mer space; both are taken up in
04_align. Column order, project, country, coordinates and call count per run
are in samples_order.tsv.
