FULL CAPTION

Every one of the 1,347 field-collected Triticum aestivum RNA-seq runs is one
column, grouped into 4 regions and ordered west to east; within a region,
columns run by longitude. 40 countries are represented across 22 BioProjects
and 1,062 of the runs carry coordinates; the country-to-region mapping is in
data/inputs/country_regions.tsv and a country missing from it stops the figure
rather than falling into an Other bucket. Runs with no location at all are not
drawn, the x axis being geography, and they stay in supp_data_s5 and in every
count the module makes.

EVERY WHEAT PATHOGEN DETECTED GETS A ROW, however rare: 14 were called and 14
are drawn, banded by the disease they cause. There is no prevalence threshold
on the rows, so a single call is as visible as a thousand and the reader can
see which organisms are at the thin end. The 1 not drawn is A. alternata (2):
it is collapsed into the Alternaria genus row, that genus being drawn as one
unresolved organism rather than as twenty congeners, and the genus is a
resident. Its calls are in supp_data_s5.

RUNS CARRYING ONLY THEIR DECLARED PATHOGEN ARE DRAWN, and so are runs carrying
no wheat pathogen at all. Of 1,347 columns, 696 hold one call and it is the
declared one, 94 hold none, and 549 hold at least one undeclared pathogen.
Dropping the first two groups would raise the undeclared share from 41% to
almost all of it, which is the arithmetic of conditioning a denominator on its
own outcome. A run with no call is not an empty run: it carries a resident
community with no single species concentrated enough to clear the floors.

RESIDENTS HAVE NO ROW OF THEIR OWN: 66 resident taxa are called over 4,034
calls, and the single ramp at the foot of the panel carries them as a count
per run. That is a decision about space rather than about evidence — the
detection-space panels share a page with this one — and the ramp counts every
resident call. Its scale is three-stop rather than two because the counts are
bimodal: 47% of runs call no resident and a second cluster sits in the teens,
and a pale-to-mid-green ramp put both in the top half of the lightness range.
The pathogens have no matching ramp, deliberately: they have rows, so a ramp
would restate a column the reader can already count. The plant host is not
drawn either: every library has one, so it is a ranking question rather than a
detection one, and supp_data_s5 carries the ranked host for all 1,347 runs.

THE TOP ROW IS THE STUDY, NOT AN ORGANISM. 9 of the 31 cohort projects are
virus surveys, and the 108 runs they contributed are marked there in the same
grey the declared calls use, because it is the same kind of fact: what the
study reported. They declare no fungus because they were not looking for one,
which is why the panel's largest block of columns with no declared pathogen is
theirs, and why those columns also carry the fewest calls — their libraries
are about half the depth of the rest. Without the marker that block reads as
missing curation, which it is not: every project in the cohort now has a
curated declaration. `study_target` is curated from each paper's own statement
of aim and is published in supp_data_s2; it is not inferred from the
declaration being empty.

Colour carries two things and neither is abundance. The HUE is the taxon's
category in the reference matrix, the same hue it has in 02_reference's matrix
and tree. The TREATMENT is who knew: full colour where the study that
generated the run did not declare that organism, charcoal where it did. There
is no third state, because every study drawn here now has a curated
declaration.

Counting all 14 wheat pathogens and not only the 14 given a row, there are
1,239 declared calls and 767 undeclared. 549 runs carry at least one
undeclared wheat pathogen; in 527 of them it sits beside the pathogen the
study was about, and in 22 it is the only wheat pathogen present. The first is
the common case and follows from the declared targets being recovered in
nearly every run; the second is the stronger one, a study that reported no
pathogen we can detect and whose reads carry one anyway.

PATHOGENS OF OTHER PLANTS ARE NOT DRAWN. 5 were called over 11 calls — P.
teres (6), P. lingam (2), S. lycopersici (1), A. lentis (1), B. cinerea (1),
runs in brackets. They span coverage 0.023 to 0.117, and 5 of the 11 sit at or
below 0.034, the highest the pure-fungal control's own artefact reached; the
largest, at 0.117, does not, so this is a weak set rather than a demonstrably
spurious one. Four of the five are Pleosporales, the order holding the wheat
spot and blotch pathogens, and each was called in a run where one of those was
present. That is too tenuous to draw as presence on a panel whose other rows
are not, and it is the question release/04_align answers from species-unique
reads rather than the question this screen can. They are in supp_data_s5.
Their scarcity is still the db2 arm working: under db1' these were the
conspicuous artefact, reads from a Pleosporaceae with no close reference
forced onto a tomato or soybean pathogen, and adding the predicted competitors
gave them somewhere nearer to go.

The unit is the run and not the BioProject, deliberately. Projects in this
cohort are not geographically coherent — the largest spans twelve countries —
so grouping by project averages a worldwide survey into one value and hides
the axis along which the complex varies.

That axis is visible without any statistics. Z. tritici is called in Europe
34% and in Americas 5%, Africa 5%, Asia 0%: 34% of 825 European runs against
4% of 522 runs from everywhere else, which is the published distribution of
Septoria tritici blotch. The pattern is recovered from reads collected for
other reasons, and it is evidence the calls are infections rather than
contamination: neither spore drift, index hopping nor cross-mapping is
organised by continent.

Detection is presence, not activity, and a species-level call inside a densely
represented genus is a call on that genus' k-mer space; both are taken up in
04_align, and the congener case is shown in the detection-space panels beside
this one. Column order, project, country, region, coordinates and call count
per run are in samples_cols.tsv.
