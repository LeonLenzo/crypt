FULL CAPTION

Geographic, compositional and temporal distribution of the field cereal RNA-seq
cohort: 7,630 publicly archived samples from 39 BioProjects, each curated to a
field setting against a quoted sentence in an available manuscript. Colour marks
the crop throughout.

(a) Map. One point per locality and crop. Point area is proportional to the
LOGARITHM of the number of samples there, so a larger point means more samples
but area is not proportional to count. Points are semi-transparent, so
overlapping localities compound into darker patches. Country outlines are drawn
in the background colour.

  Circles, marked "Locality" in the Resolution key (7,470 samples at
  462 localities), sit at coordinates geocoded from the locality string
  recorded for the sample.

  Diamonds, marked "Country" (147 samples across 23 countries), sit at the
  centroid of the largest landmass polygon of their country. These samples name
  a country and nothing finer, so the diamond states WHICH COUNTRY and is drawn
  at a place nobody sampled. It is not a collection site and should not be read
  as one.

(b) Samples per crop and (c) distinct localities per crop. The same cohort
counted two ways. Axis values are scaled by the factor given in the axis title,
so a label of 3 in (b) is 3,000 samples and a label of 3 in (c) is 300
localities. Localities are distinct locality strings finer than a country, so
the bare-country samples add to (b) and not to (c), and repeated sampling of one
site across seasons counts once. (c) therefore counts localities the studies
NAMED, which is slightly more than the map can draw: 11 of those names resolve
no better than their own country and appear in (a) as diamonds.

(d) Year. Samples per year of collection, stacked by crop, over 2012 to 2024.
Only the first and last year are labelled, to give the range; every year in
between has its own bar. Based on the 7,379 of 7,630 samples carrying a
collection date. Bars rather than a continuous series, because year here is a
discrete count: an area or line would draw a slope between 2015 and 2016
asserting collection at every instant in between, and none occurred. Every year
in the range has a position on the axis, so a year with no collection reads as
an absent bar rather than being skipped.

The crops are not comparable in shape and the two bar panels exist to show it:
rice is 2,784 samples at 6 localities because the rice data are large
GWAS panels grown at one farm, while wheat is 1,215 at 420 because the
wheat data are rust surveys that sample broadly. (a) alone reads as a wheat map
and (b) alone reads as a rice cohort; both are true.


WHAT IS NOT SHOWN

13 of the 7,630 samples carry no location at all and are absent from the map
while remaining in (b), (c) and (d).

The 147 samples drawn at country centroids are not a geocoding failure. Most are
rust surveys where the submitter recorded the country an isolate came from and
no collection site, and ENA holds no latitude, longitude or locality field for
them. Where a finer value exists it has been pursued: supplementary tables were
imported for the Ada21 projects and PRJEB36485, Lewis et al. 2024 Dataset S2
moved 45 of PRJEB65589's samples from a country to a named site, and
seeds/missing_localities.tsv is the worklist of localities that do not place,
and carries 57 reviewed corrections for names the gazetteer could not match
as written. The rows still without a correction are open work.

Three crops below a 100-sample floor are excluded from every panel: barley (3), other cereal (55).
Barley's absence is a property of the archive, not of this figure - there is no
field barley study in the cohort, only incidental barley samples inside wheat
rust surveys. Note that the kraken read-based host call reports far more barley
than this; it is wrong, and systematically so, in exactly these rust surveys.
See seeds/found.tsv, 2026-10-09.

Localities are placed at four precisions, recorded per locality in
data/gold/cereal_localities.tsv: 344 samples at a name that resolved as given,
63 after a research-facility name was dropped (for example "USA: Nebraska,
Lincoln (UNL Havelock Research Farm)" placed as Lincoln, Nebraska), 55
through a reviewed spelling correction, and 1 at province level.

(e) Study design. The share of each crop's samples that were sampled WITH
REFERENCE TO DISEASE - chosen because diseased, deliberately infected, sprayed
to suppress disease, or chosen because NOT diseased - against those sampled on
some other criterion entirely: genotype, treatment, growth stage, clock time.
Pathology share by crop: wheat 98%, maize 9%, rice 0%, sorghum 0%.

This is a result, not a caveat. Crop and design are not confounded in the
estimable sense; they are SEPARATED, and no model recovers a comparison that
was never sampled. It is also why this chapter applies two evidence standards
rather than one. A wheat sample arrives with a pathogen already declared, so a
finding there has to be a SECOND organism; a rice or sorghum sample has none
declared, so a single unreported pathogen is itself the finding.

Read from the curated `sampling_selection` field alone, which is 100%
paper-sourced: no run's value comes from an archive attribute. The label is the
SAMPLING criterion rather than the study's purpose, and the two diverge in one
place - PRJNA564795 is a gray leaf spot trial at a disease hotspot whose 10 runs
were sampled without regard to individual symptom, so they count as physiology
here while the study is pathology. 3 samples record no selection at all and
are drawn as a third, invisible segment rather than dropped. Run cohort.py for
the full cross-tab.

Distribution of 7,630 field cereal RNA-seq samples from 39 BioProjects, coloured
by crop. (a) One point per locality and crop, point area proportional to log
sample count. Circles are 7,470 samples at 462 geocoded localities;
