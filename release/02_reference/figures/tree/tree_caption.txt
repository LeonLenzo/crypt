FULL CAPTION

Taxonomic distribution of the 1,300 reference taxa that have both a USDA
record and a gene model, downloaded or predicted, drawn as a circular
cladogram built from the NCBI taxonomy. IT IS A TAXONOMY, NOT A PHYLOGENY:
radius encodes rank, from kingdom at the centre to species at the rim, and
carries no information about divergence. Branch lengths are not evolutionary
distances and must not be read as any.

Only taxa USDA records under their own species name appear. A taxon admitted
because its GENUS was recorded has no tip, since it is there to give the
classifier a competitor and is never reported as a detection, and a taxon with
no gene model has none either, since it contributes no CDS. These 1,300 are
the taxa the database is actually built from.

Bar length is the number of genome assemblies for that taxon on a log scale,
with dotted gridlines and numerals at 1, 10 and 100. The spread is wide: 936
of 1,300 taxa have a single assembly and the largest has 435.

Bar colour is the taxon's cell in panel A, using the same five colours, so a
hue means the same thing in both panels:

  wheat pathogen                                25
  pathogen elsewhere, recorded on wheat         25
  non-pathogen, recorded on wheat              204
  pathogen elsewhere, not on wheat              63
  non-pathogen, not on wheat                   983

The inner ring at the base of each bar is the share of that taxon's assemblies
carrying a gene model: pale where all of them do, dark purple where few do.
620 of 1,300 taxa are fully annotated and 680 are not. The dark segments are
the gene-prediction worklist, because those are genomes already deposited
whose CDS the database cannot currently use.

The outer ring marks the 128 taxa whose gene model was PREDICTED here rather
than downloaded (research/predict, ab initio, validated against BUSCO and
against an annotated congener's k-mer space). Every one of them had no NCBI
annotation at all, which is both why it was on the worklist and why its inner
ring is at the dark end: the bar beside a mark is entirely ours, and the taxon
was absent from this panel before db2 because an unannotated genome
contributes nothing to classify against. Where they went:

  wheat pathogen                                 1
  pathogen elsewhere, recorded on wheat          4
  non-pathogen, recorded on wheat               96
  pathogen elsewhere, not on wheat              17
  non-pathogen, not on wheat                    10

The marks are not scattered evenly, and that is the worklist showing: they
concentrate where a taxon is recorded on wheat and nobody had annotated it.

The 5 arcs outside the bars mark the taxonomic classes large enough to label,
each identified by a SYMBOL rather than a name: circle Agaricomycetes (206
taxa); square Dothideomycetes (203 taxa); diamond Eurotiomycetes (141 taxa);
triangle Leotiomycetes (77 taxa); inverted triangle Sordariomycetes (420
taxa). A symbol is used because a class name set radially reads at a different
angle in every quadrant and competes with the bars it annotates. The arcs are
reading aids for position in the tree, not a result, and the smaller classes
are present as tips but carry no arc.


HOW THE PREDICTED TAXA WERE CHOSEN

All 135 predicted assemblies, the 128 marked here and the rest on taxa that
have no tip, were selected by a rule applied to the reference table and never
picked by hand. research/predict/worklist.py is a SELECTION over
supp_data_s3_reference.tsv, and its result is published as
data/curation/extra_cds_db2.tsv, one row per predicted assembly carrying the
tier it qualified under and the reason. Three tiers, in priority order:

  A    118  SILENT AND REPORTABLE. USDA records the species on wheat, or
            PHI-base scores it a pathogen of something, and it has no gene
            model at all. A detection would be reported for such a taxon and
            the database cannot produce one. Led by Fusarium asiaticum, a
            wheat pathogen with 259 assemblies and not one of them annotated.

  B      3  DARK GENUS RECORDED ON WHEAT. One genome per genus that USDA
            records on wheat and that carries no annotation anywhere in it,
            for the genera tier A does not already reach. A genus with no gene
            model gives a read from it no neighbour to land on at all.

  C2    14  CONGENER COMPETITOR. A callable wheat-relevant taxon whose genus
            holds no annotated congener gets one predicted, so that a read
            from an absent relative has somewhere to go other than onto the
            taxon itself.

ONE ASSEMBLY PER SPECIES. A second assembly of the same species adds about a
sixth of its k-mer space and less again thereafter, so at a fixed compute
budget breadth beats depth and no species was predicted twice.

WHICH ASSEMBLY, where a species has several: plausible genome size first (at
least 5 Mb), then assembly level, then fewest contigs. Level cannot come first
because assembly_level is a submitter label and nothing validates it.
GCA_986280995.1 is 528 bp on a single contig, registered as a Complete Genome,
and a level-first sort ranked it above a 46.7 Mb scaffold assembly for the
same species.

ONE GENE FINDER, for comparability. Every prediction is ab initio from the
same tool, never mixed with homology projection from a related species. Two
finders carry two bias profiles, which would make two taxa's minimizer
denominators differ for a reason that is not biological, and nothing in the
table would flag it.

WHAT WAS DELIBERATELY NOT PREDICTED, and this is the decision that shaped the
list. An earlier worklist took every species whose genus holds no annotation
at all: 932 species in 437 genera as the reference then stood. Ranked by
species count those genera are Leccinum, Inocybe, Xerocomus and Parmotrema,
ectomycorrhizal and lichenised fungi that do not occur in a wheat leaf, and
only 13 of the 437 contain a species USDA records on wheat. Predicting all of
them would have spent several times the compute to annotate organisms this
cohort cannot meet. The tiers above are what remains once that is recognised.


HOW TO READ IT

The panel is mostly one pale colour on purpose. The reference is a community,
and the organisms anyone has studied are a sliver of it: the pathogens are the
few bright spikes against a pale field, and they are tallest where they are
hyper-sequenced, which is a statement about research attention and not about
abundance in a field.


WHAT IS NOT SHOWN

Nothing here is a detection. Bar length counts deposited assemblies, so a tall
bar means a well-sequenced organism, never an abundant or a frequently
detected one.

Taxa with no gene model are absent from this panel but remain in the database
scope and in panel A, where the gap between its Asm. and CDS counts measures
them, and its Pred. count is the part of that gap this arm closed.
