─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT THIS FIGURE SHOWS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A heatmap of co-infection pairs where the secondary pathogen was detected on
a host not previously recorded in PHI-base for that pathogen. Each cell is
one (host species, secondary pathogen) combination; fill colour = number of
BioSamples with that combination. Only the top 35 pathogens × top 30 hosts
by frequency are shown.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FILTER
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Source: runs.tsv, biosample_representative = True,
        interaction_status = "novel_host_range"
~590 BioSamples with novel_host_range status.

interaction_status = "novel_host_range" means:
  - The secondary pathogen is present in PHI-base (known pathogen)
  - BUT the specific (pathogen, host) pair is not recorded in PHI-base
  - i.e. pathogen is known to infect plants, but not this plant species
  - This is the primary signal of interest for the paper hypothesis

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
AXES
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
X: secondary pathogen name (binomial, or f. sp. where applicable; viruses
   kept as full name). Sorted by frequency descending (left = most frequent).
Y: host species (binomial). Sorted by frequency descending (top = most hosts).
Fill: number of BioSamples; cells with ≥ 3 BioSamples are annotated with the
count. White/grey cells indicate pairs below the top-N cutoff or absent.
Colour scale: #fde8d8 (low) → #c0392b (high).

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT TO LOOK FOR
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Pathogens with broad host range signals (many filled cells across multiple
  host rows) — suggest genuine host range expansion or sampling bias.
- Hosts with many novel co-infections detected (many filled cells in a host
  row) — may indicate high co-infection susceptibility or intensive sampling.
- Highly repeated combinations (dark cells) are candidates for follow-up:
  multiple independent BioSamples from different BioProjects finding the same
  novel interaction is a stronger signal than a single study.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CAVEATS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- "Novel" is relative to PHI-base curation depth — PHI-base is not exhaustive,
  and some apparent novel interactions may be published but not yet curated.
  Cross-referencing with PMID and methods text (from 03b_fetch_literature.py)
  is recommended before claiming novelty.
- Includes all co-infection confidence levels (same_genus_secondary not
  filtered here). High-confidence (diff-genus) subset would be a subset.
- STAT k-mer evidence only — not validated by alignment or PCR. Counts
  represent computational detections, not confirmed infections.
- Virus names retained as-is (not truncated to binomial); virus rows reflect
  ICTV plant virus detections via the viral STAT kingdom.
