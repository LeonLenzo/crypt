Geographic distribution of fungal pathogens detected in 958 classified wheat libraries
that carry field coordinates, across 385 localities in 36 countries. Each panel is one
species, and a locality is marked only where that species was detected in a library whose
study DID NOT declare it, so every coloured point is an unreported detection.

That restriction matters rather than being a formality. Puccinia triticina is the declared
target of one project and an undeclared bystander in eight others; mapping both together
would draw the sampling design instead of the biology.

BOTH SURVIVORS ARE Pyrenophora, AND THEY ARE NOT EACH OTHER. A genus-level coincidence
in this pipeline is first a congener-smearing suspect, so it was checked: of the 60
libraries calling either species, only 3 call both, 40 are tan spot alone and 17 net
blotch alone. Smearing produces near-complete overlap at near-identical coverage, which is
what the Alternaria cluster does; these are independent, and where each is detected alone
the median coverages differ (7.0% against 2.7%). Two stubble-borne Pleosporales sharing a
continental distribution is coherent rather than suspicious. P. teres is primarily a
BARLEY pathogen and its presence on wheat, while recorded, is worth flagging.

Pyrenophora tritici-repentis is detected in 1 of 350 maritime libraries and 15 of 292
continental ones, an eighteenfold difference, and tan spot is indeed a disease of
reduced-tillage continental wheat rather than of the maritime northwest. Everything else
fails, INCLUDING the two gradients the panels make most obvious: Zymoseptoria tritici
reads as strongly maritime and is 42.3% against 34.3% at p = 0.04, which does not survive
correction, and Microdochium nivale looks almost exclusively British and is 9.1% against
7.2% at p = 0.39. Both are
mostly the sampling: 354 of the 648 European libraries are maritime, and 317 of those are
from the UK alone.

POINT SIZE is the number of libraries at that locality in which the species was detected,
not a rate. Most localities contributed a single library, so a rate there would be 0 or
100% and would overstate what one sample can say.

COORDINATE HANDLING. 140 of 1,098 classified wheat libraries are not drawn:
   129  no coordinates
    10  precision 'country'
     1  precision 'region'
Libraries whose location resolves only to a country or region are excluded rather than
placed at a centroid. On a map about where disease was found, a centroid invents a field
site at a place nobody sampled.

WHAT THIS IS NOT. Every library was collected because the plant was diseased, so these
are not prevalence surfaces. A locality with no coloured point was sampled and the species
was not detected there, which under a disease-conditioned design is weaker than absence.

SHORT CAPTION

Undeclared fungal pathogens across 385 wheat sampling localities in 36 countries.
Each panel is one species; coloured points mark localities where it was detected in a
library whose study did not report it, sized by the number of such libraries. Open grey
points are every sampled locality and are repeated in each panel, so an empty region can
be read as sampled-and-not-found rather than unsampled. Detections are conditioned on
visible disease and are not prevalence estimates.
