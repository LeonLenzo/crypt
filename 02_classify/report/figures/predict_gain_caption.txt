FULL CAPTION

CDS annotations available to db_v4 by route into scope. The SOLID bar is the 4,271
assemblies that carry an NCBI gene model and are therefore already in the database. The
PALE bar is the 12,806 assemblies that carry NO gene model and could therefore receive a
predicted one — every unannotated assembly, whether or not its species already has an
annotated one elsewhere.

TWO DIFFERENT QUESTIONS, and this figure answers the second:
  how many SPECIES become reachable?        2,138 (species with no annotation at all)
  how many gene models could we MAKE?       12,806 (every unannotated assembly)
The first is the breadth argument -- 1,547 species now, 3,685 possible. The second is the
depth argument, and it is larger because well-sampled species carry many unannotated
assemblies: more assemblies per species widens pangenome coverage without adding taxa.
Routes, colours and the precedence rule are shared with the db_v4 ring figure; each
species is counted once, under the strongest evidence that applies.

  route                                           CDS now   addable     total
  pathogen recorded on wheat, rice, maize or sorghum     893     4,191     5,084
  plant pathogen, not recorded on those crops      1,444     3,488     4,932
  host associate: seen in a phytobiome study         244       629       873
  plant associate: recorded on a plant             1,117     2,683     3,800
  congener: shares a genus with a pathogen           537       743     1,280
  TOTAL                                            4,271    12,806    17,077

THE ADDITION IS NOT HYPOTHETICAL. Measured on 60 fungal assemblies that already carry
an NCBI gene model, so the annotation serves as ground truth, ab-initio prediction
recovers a median 96% of the true CDS k-mer space at 94% precision.
On 59 assemblies WITHOUT a gene model, a median 96% of the predicted k-mer
space is absent from db_v4 — so the pale bars are close to their full face value and
not largely redundant with what the database already holds.

THE host ROUTE IS NOT DRAWN: 6 plant host species, 36 annotations and 1,072 technically
predictable assemblies. Those 1,072 are excluded because we would never predict them --
db_v4 already holds 43 host CDS assemblies across the six species, which is ample for
host competition, and a plant needs the Streptophyta model rather than the Fungi one.
Drawn, it would be the largest pale bar in the figure and would represent work nobody
intends to do. The row stays in predict_gain.tsv.

WHAT THE BAR DOES NOT SAY. Annotations, not sequence: a pale bar counts assemblies that
would gain a gene model, not the k-mer space they contribute, and the two are not
proportional. Nor is every addable species equally worth adding — novelty against db_v4
ranges from 0.05 for species in densely sampled genera (Penicillium, Alternaria) to 0.99
for genera the database does not represent at all, which is where the phyllosphere
residents sit. Prediction quality also falls on large repeat-rich genomes, where
ab-initio calls genes inside transposons.

SHORT CAPTION

CDS annotations reachable by db_v4, by route into scope. Solid bars are the 4,271
assemblies carrying an NCBI gene model; pale bars are the 12,806 unannotated assemblies that
ab-initio prediction could annotate. Of those, the subset belonging to species with no
annotation at all would add 2,138 new species to the 1,547 already present; the remainder
deepen species the database already holds. Routes and colours are shared with the db_v4
ring, each species counted once under the strongest evidence that applies.
Prediction recovers a median 96% of true CDS k-mer space at 94% precision on
species with a known annotation, and a median 96% of what it predicts for unannotated
species is absent from db_v4.
