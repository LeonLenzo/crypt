FULL CAPTION

Composition of the reference database: 5,843 fungal taxa carrying 21,413
genome assemblies, of which 5,302 have a gene model and can contribute CDS.
Each taxon is placed by two independent facts and the matrix crosses them.
Rows are whether the USDA Plant-Fungal Database names that species on wheat or
does not. Columns are what PHI-base scores: no pathogenicity record, a named
disease on some plant, or a named disease on wheat. Cells are disjoint and sum
to the cohort: every taxon is in exactly one.

Each cell reports four counts. Taxa is distinct NCBI taxa; Asm. is the genome
assemblies available for them; CDS is the subset of those assemblies carrying
a gene model DOWNLOADED FROM NCBI, which is what the database could be built
from before this work. The gap between Asm. and CDS is the annotation
bottleneck that motivates gene prediction.

Pred. is the fourth count and is the part of that gap this work closed: gene
models PREDICTED here, ab initio, for taxa NCBI has never annotated
(research/predict; validated against BUSCO and against an annotated congener's
k-mer space). All 135 are taxa whose NCBI annotation count is zero, so Pred.
never overlaps CDS and a predicted taxon was uncallable before db2. 101 of the
135 are in the wheat row, which is where the worklist aimed them: a fungus
USDA records on wheat, in the reference, with no gene model is the most
expensive kind of silence this database can hold.

Colour carries both axes, so the panel needs no key. Hue is the column: green
for no pathogenicity record, amber for a pathogen of other plants, orange-red
for a wheat pathogen. Saturation is the row: full strength where USDA names
the taxon on wheat, pale where it does not. Panel B uses the same five colours
for the same five cells.


WHAT THE EMPTY CELL MEANS

One of the six combinations does not occur and is NOT DRAWN. No wheat pathogen
in scope is off-wheat in USDA: all 25 sit in the top row. USDA host records
and PHI-base pathogenicity scores are independent compilations made for
different purposes, so their agreeing here is an empirical check on the scope
rule rather than a definition, and it is the test the design has to pass. The
gap is a result and not a layout choice.

A second structural absence is real but is no longer visible as a gap, because
the rows no longer separate it. Of the 3,465 taxa that entered the reference
on their GENUS rather than under their own name, not one carries a pathogen
label of either kind. A fungus PHI-base scores with a named disease is a
studied fungus and USDA records studied fungi, so the label cannot reach a
taxon nobody has recorded. No detection can be called a pathogen on genus
membership alone.


HOW THE TWO AXES ARE DEFINED

The unit is a TAXON, not a described species, and the panel says so. 1,373 of
the 3,465 genus-only taxa are NCBI `sp.` nodes: unidentified isolates carrying
a strain code and no binomial. They are legitimate reference entries, because
an unidentified Alternaria gives the classifier as real a competitor as a
named one, but they are not species in any taxonomic sense. The observation
rows do not have the problem (14 of 2,378), because USDA indexes only formal
names.

THE PLANT ROW POOLS TWO ROUTES INTO THE REFERENCE AND DOES NOT SEPARATE THEM,
deliberately. 3,465 of its taxa are there because USDA recorded their GENUS
and not the species itself. How a taxon reached the reference is a fact about
the database build, recorded per taxon in the `route` column of
supp_data_s3_reference.tsv, and drawing it as a row made the figure assert
something about the ORGANISM that the route cannot support: USDA Fungus-Host
is a herbarium records database, so an unrecorded name is as likely to be a
gap in its coverage as a fact about the fungus. Two cases arrive by that route
and nothing separates them: 2,865 taxa whose genus is represented here by a
USDA-recorded sibling species, and 600 whose genus qualified on USDA records
that could not be built into a row of their own, mostly lichens, mushrooms and
mycorrhizas with no annotated assembly. Treating the route as weak evidence
would discard real detections, and a draft of a downstream figure did exactly
that: it dropped *Dioszegia hungarica*, a common wheat phyllosphere yeast USDA
holds only under its former name *Bullera*.

A PHI-base row counts only where it NAMES A DISEASE. PHI-base scores an
interaction against whatever host the experiment used, and the plant is not
always the host being harmed: in a tripartite insect-fungus-plant assay, or an
endophyte colonisation study, the plant is present but is not the victim, and
those rows carry Disease_name = "no data found". 5 fungi in this reference
were labelled plant pathogens on such rows alone and are not labelled here
(Aspergillus ochraceus, Epichloe festucae, Leptographium clavigerum,
Metarhizium acridum, Metarhizium robertsii). Each is a plant associate rather
than a pathogen. The condition applies to the wheat column too, where it
changes nothing.

The wheat pathogen label covers 26 taxa: 18 from PHI-base and 8 from a curated
supplement (data/inputs/wheat_pathogens_curated.tsv) carrying the canonical
wheat diseases PHI-base does not curate. 25 of them are in the reference. The
remaining 1 (Gaeumannomyces graminis) has no in-scope assembly, so it carries
the label but is not in the reference and does not appear in the panel.
Provenance is recorded per taxon in wheat_pathogen_source; no call is
anonymous.

Before counting, 16 provisional NCBI names were folded onto the USDA binomial
they belong to and 1 was promoted in place, moving 63 assemblies. USDA and
NCBI can hold one fungus under two names, which otherwise splits it into an
observation with no genome and a genus-only row with no host record. 5 `aff.`
nodes were deliberately NOT merged, because `aff.` denotes a related
undescribed species and those belong in the genus-only row. Every decision is
a row in data/curation/screen_merges.tsv.


WHAT IS NOT SHOWN

Scope is ANY plant, never wheat. The database deliberately holds fungi with no
wheat record and no pathogenicity record, because a reference absent from the
database is invisible: its reads go silently to the nearest taxon present. The
columns are annotations applied to a detection, never filters on the database.

The panel counts what the reference CONTAINS, not what was detected. No cell
is a claim about abundance, prevalence or co-infection.
